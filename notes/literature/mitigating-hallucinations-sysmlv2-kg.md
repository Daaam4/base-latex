## Introduction

- Describes the increasing complexity of modern systems and the need for automation in systems engineering.
- Introduces Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) and SysML v2 as solutions, but highlights their limitations.
- Discusses the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automating formal modeling, but emphasizes the challenge of hallucinations.
- Presents two key research questions focusing on using a tri-layered knowledge graph (KG) architecture to mitigate hallucinations and improve semantic fidelity in LLM-generated SysML v2 models.

## Tri-Layered Knowledge Graphs with Prompt Template Alignment

- Discusses the challenges of reuse in LLM-based system modeling, particularly the difficulties in constructing accurate and comprehensive prompts for complex systems.
- Introduces a tri-layered KG architecture and its integration with reusable prompt templates to address these challenges.
- Describes **Layer 1:** SysML Characterization Knowledge Graph — encodes reusable SysML patterns and annotations for nine diagram types.
- Describes **Layer 2:** Domain-Specific Knowledge Graph — extends Layer 1 with domain-specific constructs relevant to sectors like aerospace and automotive engineering.
- Describes **Layer 3:** System-Specific Knowledge Graph — automatically generated by parsing structured requirements and aligning them with Layers 1 and 2.
- Explains how the tri-layered KG structure populates prompt templates, enabling the generation of context-aware, domain-specific SysML v2 artifacts.

## Case Study: Engineering of Satellite System

- Presents a case study focusing on the engineering of a satellite system to evaluate the framework.
- Briefly outlines the process of generating a system-specific knowledge graph (Layer 3) for the satellite system, which includes:
  - Preparing input data from capability-linked requirements.
  - Recommending SysML diagrams based on system features.
  - Retrieving information from Layers 1 and 2.
  - Generating SysML definitions for each requirement.
  - Constructing the Layer 3 KG.
  - Assembling system-level prompts.
  - Compiling diagrams into a unified model.
  - Validating and handling errors in the generated SysML v2 code.
  - Building out subsystem-level models.

## Assessment of the Results

- Highlights the lack of a verifiable ground truth dataset for SysML v2 models and explains the creation of a manually curated dataset for evaluation.
- Notes the two primary objectives of the evaluation: verifying structural validity and assessing semantic accuracy.
- Mentions five evaluation categories: Average Structure Score (AStrS), Average Manual Structure Score (AMSS), Average Semantic Score (ASemS), Consistency Ratio (CR), and prompt type analysis.
- Discusses potential threats to validity, including expert bias, limited dataset size, and the challenges of evaluating semantic correctness.

## Discussion

- Summarizes the evaluation findings, emphasizing the framework's ability to produce structurally valid models.
- Acknowledges the limitations regarding semantic fidelity and output determinism.
- Discusses how the tri-layered KG architecture mitigates hallucinations and ensures structural integrity, addressing RQ-1.
- Presents evidence that incorporating capability-linked requirements and structured prompting improves semantic accuracy and traceability, addressing RQ-2.

## Related Work

- Reviews existing research on LLM-based code and model generation, highlighting the issue of hallucinations.
- Discusses previous approaches to SysML model automation, including rule-based methods, NLP-based pipelines, and KG integration.
- Mentions studies demonstrating the benefits and limitations of using KGs to enhance LLM reasoning.
- Notes the need for improved benchmarking tools and datasets for evaluating LLM-generated SysML v2 models.

## Conclusion and Outlook

- Concludes that the tri-layered KG framework provides a promising approach to enhance LLM-assisted generation of SysML v2 models.
- Reiterates the challenges of semantic precision and output determinism.
- Highlights the framework's ability to support robust, traceable, and semantically aligned workflows in DE and MBSE.
- Outlines future research directions, including developing automated validation mechanisms, more comprehensive benchmarking tools, expanding the curated dataset, and improving output determinism.
