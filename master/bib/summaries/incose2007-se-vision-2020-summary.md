# Systems Engineering Vision 2020

## Topic
A forward-looking INCOSE technical product projecting the state of practice of systems engineering to the year 2020, intended as "the authoritative source of a shared vision for the global systems engineering community." It analyses five focus areas — Global Systems Engineering Environment, Systems and their Nature, Systems Engineering Processes, Models and Model-Based Systems Engineering, and Systems Engineering Education — moving from current-state assessment through drivers/inhibitors to a projected future state. Its central, most-cited contribution is the definitional passage that coins Model-Based Systems Engineering: "the formalized application of modeling to support system requirements, design, analysis, verification and validation activities beginning in the conceptual design phase and continuing throughout development and later life cycle phases." It positions MBSE as part of a broader model-centric trend already underway in mechanical, electrical and software engineering, and explicitly frames it as displacing the document-centric approach that dominated prior systems-engineering practice (Figure 5's "past vs. future" contrast).

## Method
Not an empirical study: the document is a foresight/visioning technical report, synthesised from INCOSE workshop outputs (the January 2004 Technical Vision Workshop, January 2006 Vision 2020 Workshop focus groups, April 2006 Research Vision Workshop, July 2006 Modeling and Simulation Vision Workshop) plus review input from partner engineering societies (IET, SESA, AFIS, AIAA, SOLE, EuSEC). Its "evaluation" is qualitative: current-state description (2007) of MBSE as ad hoc, tool-fragmented and hindered by absent convergent standards (citing the then-emerging OMG SysML and ISO 10303-233/AP233), followed by a 2020 projection describing MBSE's key future characteristics — domain-specific modeling languages, mathematically-grounded modeling standards, reusable model libraries, distributed model repositories, and secure published-interface data exchange (Figure 8, "Cross-domain model integration").

## Relevance
This is the canonical definitional source for MBSE that the thesis's own vocabulary (Abfragen/Validierung/Verifizierung/Korrektur/Erstellen use cases as instances of "requirements, design, analysis, verification and validation activities") ultimately traces back to — nearly every downstream MBSE paper cites this definition directly or indirectly, so it anchors the thesis's opening framing rather than any specific use case. Two details matter for the design chapter: (1) the 2007 diagnosis that tool fragmentation and lack of standards were the chief inhibitors to MBSE adoption is the structural problem an MCP bridge (a standardized, tool-agnostic interface) is a 2020s-era instance of addressing; (2) the 2020 vision explicitly separates "verification" and "validation" as distinct MBSE activities from the outset, supporting the thesis's decision to treat *Verifizierung* and *Validierung* as separate use cases rather than a single V&V category (consistent with the ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 definitions the thesis also cites).
