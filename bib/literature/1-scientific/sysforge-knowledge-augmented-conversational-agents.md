## Introduction

- Describes the importance of Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) and the challenges in automatically generating accurate models using SysML v2.
- Notes the potential of LLMs for automating engineering design processes but highlights limitations of current approaches due to the novelty of SysML v2 and its limited representation in training data.
- Mentions the limitations of basic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks in capturing complex system relationships.
- Introduces **SysForge**, a novel framework designed to address the challenges of generating accurate and consistent SysML v2 models by transforming the LLM into a collaborative design partner.

## Related Works

- Discusses the use of generative AI in systems engineering, highlighting DeHart et al.'s work on conversational interaction with SysML v2 models using LLMs.
- Reviews the application of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for code generation, noting the limitations of standard RAG in handling code dependencies and highlighting the need for a dependency-aware approach.
- Presents advanced RAG frameworks like CodeRAG and knowledge graph-based approaches that address dependency issues in code generation.

## The SysForge Framework

- Provides a detailed explanation of the SysForge framework, outlining its architecture and workflow.
- Describes the three key stages of the framework: Knowledge Graph Construction and Retrieval, Structured Context Synthesis, and Iterative Refinement and Validation.
- Explains the role of the Conversational Agent, the SysML Knowledge Base (SysML-KB), the Context Synthesizer Agent, the Designer Agent, and the Validator Agent within the workflow.
- Discusses the process of encoding code and comments into vector embeddings for efficient retrieval and the hybrid search method used for retrieving relevant code snippets.

## Experiments

- Details the experimental setup, including the dataset, partitioning of data, and evaluation metrics used (Pass@1 and BLEU).
- Presents the comparative evaluation results for SysForge against a standard LLM approach and a semantic RAG baseline.
- Discusses the performance improvements achieved by SysForge, highlighting the effectiveness of dependency-aware retrieval, context synthesis, and the iterative refinement loop.
- Shows a visualization of SysML v2 code generated by SysForge, further validating its accuracy and consistency.

## Conclusion and Future Work

- Summarizes the key contributions of SysForge and its potential to revolutionize automated MBSE.
- Emphasizes the importance of transparency and interpretability in AI-driven design processes for building trust and enabling adoption in safety-critical domains.
- Discusses the limitations of the current framework and outlines future research directions, including enhancing numerical and parametric reasoning, incorporating multimodal data into the knowledge base, and scaling the framework for enterprise-level model repositories.
